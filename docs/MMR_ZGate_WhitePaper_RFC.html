1. Abstract

This paper specifies two entry-quality components used in a family of M15 sell-side Expert Advisors for MetaTrader 4, and reports how they behaved in a structured set of tests. MMR measures how strongly one price direction dominates across several lookback windows by comparing the steepest and shallowest linear-regression slopes. ZGate blocks new sell entries when a blend of the price z-score and the volume z-score over the last N closed bars is at or above a ceiling, so that the system does not sell into stretched strength.

Tests used the repaired FXOpen EURUSD history (2010–2026), real per-year spread statistics, an explicit commission model, unseen-year evaluation, a shuffle null for interaction searches, and a cross-market check on daily US stock and ETF data. Headline findings: (i) the base EA that contains both components is profitable in its training periods but loses in every unseen year at a 5-point commission; (ii) ZGate-style z-scores carry consistent mean-reversion information in two independent markets and two eras, so the “do not sell strength” direction is supported; (iii) MMR on EURUSD shows no stable sign of its top-versus-bottom forward-return edge across 17 years, while on daily stocks its lowest quintile shows the best forward returns in both eras; (iv) a longer sell-regime and a Hurst-trend gate reduced losses by roughly 40% but did not create profit.

3. MMR specification (Max/Min slope Ratio)

3.1 Definition

For each window length w in W = {6, 12, 18} (M15 bars), compute the OLS slope sw of the last w closes against their bar index, expressed in points per bar. Then:

MMR = max(s6, s12, s18)  /  | min(s6, s12, s18) |

Stated purpose (author): a high MMR indicates that one direction dominates across horizons, an entry-quality signal. Behaviour of the ratio: it is dimensionless, scale-free across instruments, and can be negative, zero or large when the shallowest slope is near zero.

3.2 Requirements

  • An implementation MUST use closed bars only and MUST return “undefined” (no signal) if |min slope| = 0, if fewer than two slopes are available, or if fewer than max(W)+5 bars of history exist.
  • Slopes MUST be computed on the same timeframe in which the ratio is later interpreted (M15 here).
  • Thresholds MUST be recalibrated per market: the concept is portable, the numeric cut-offs are not.

4. ZGate specification

4.1 Definition

Over the last N = ZPeriod closed bars on timeframe ZTF, let μp, σp be the mean and population standard deviation of the closes, and μv, σv those of the tick volumes. Then:

zp = (C1 − μp) / σp    zv = (V1 − μv) / σv    Z = 0.7 zp + 0.3 zv

Gate rule: a new sell entry is permitted only when Z < ZMax. If σp ≤ 0 or σv ≤ 0 the gate MUST deny the entry.

4.2 Interpretation and design intent

Z is high when price and activity are both stretched above their own recent norm. Blocking sells there avoids shorting a spike. The 70/30 weighting and ZMax = 1.0 are fitted constants (Section 9). The original price-only variant (a separate input, UseSellZGate) omits the volume term.

4.3 Dependencies and cautions

  • V is broker tick volume, which differs between feeds and between live trading and the tester’s modelled ticks; the 30% volume term is therefore not reproducible across brokers.
  • The gate is a filter: it removes entries, it does not create them.

6. Reference implementations

6.1 ZGate (MQL4, from the EA source)

if(UseZGate==1){ int n=MathMax(2,ZPeriod); double sp=0,sv=0,qp=0,qv=0;
  for(int k=1;k<=n;k++){ double c=iClose(Symbol(),ZTF,k), v=(double)iVolume(Symbol(),ZTF,k);
     sp+=c; sv+=v; qp+=c*c; qv+=v*v; }
  double mp=sp/n, mv=sv/n, dp=MathSqrt(MathMax(0,qp/n-mp*mp)), dv=MathSqrt(MathMax(0,qv/n-mv*mv));
  if(dp<=0 || dv<=0){ return(false); }
  double z=0.7*(iClose(Symbol(),ZTF,1)-mp)/dp+0.3*((double)iVolume(Symbol(),ZTF,1)-mv)/dv;
  if(z>=ZMax){ return(false); } }

6.2 MMR (Python reference)

def slope_pts(closes, i, period):          # OLS slope of closes[i-period+1..i], points per bar
    y = closes[i-period+1:i+1]; x = range(period)
    mx = sum(x)/period; my = sum(y)/period
    den = sum((xj-mx)**2 for xj in x)
    return (sum((xj-mx)*(yj-my) for xj, yj in zip(x, y))/den)*100000 if den else None

def mmr(closes, i, periods=(6, 12, 18)):
    if i < max(periods)+5: return None
    s = [slope_pts(closes, i, p) for p in periods]
    mx, mn = max(s), min(s)
    return None if mn == 0 else mx/abs(mn)

7. Methodology

  • Data. FXOpen EURUSD M1/M5/M15/…/MN history, 2010-06-23 to 2026-10. A gap in the tester’s M1 copy (200 MB versus the 302 MB source) silently skipped months in early tests; it was repaired on 2026-10-13 and all results below use the repaired history. Real spread statistics come from FXOpen M1 bid and ask files (valid from 2019; earlier files have ask equal to bid).
  • Costs. Commission confirmed from the live account: 5 pts round trip (2023–early 2024), 8 pts (2025–26), 6 pts maximum for a long period earlier. Screen tests use sp5; the audit pass uses 5 + yearly mean spread.
  • Out-of-sample discipline. Candidates are screened on the worst unseen year first, abandoned if they do not beat the base there, then evaluated on all unseen years and promoted only if they hold on at least 4 of 5 never-tested years and 2 of 3 previously seen ones.
  • Interaction searches. Pairwise tercile cells; a cell counts only if the sign of its delta versus the year average holds in at least 4 of 5 years with at least 12 trades per cell-year. A shuffle null (pnl permuted within year) estimates chance passes.
  • Cross-market check. Daily US stocks and ETFs, 36 liquid names, 2000–2026, survivor-only, no costs or dividends, overlapping 5-day windows.

8.2 MMR forward-return test on EURUSD M15 (gross of costs)

For each year, bars were sorted into MMR quintiles and the mean forward move was compared between the top and bottom quintile (edge = Q5 − Q1).

HorizonYears with edge < 0Years with edge > 0Typical size
1 hour (4 bars)12 of 175 (2010, 2012, 2018, 2023, 2026)about −2 to −7 pts

Example extremes: 2014, 1 hour, edge −16.8 pts (z −4.9); 2010, 1 day, edge +103.9 pts (z +3.5). The 1-hour gap is of the same size as the 5-point commission, and overlapping bars inflate the z-scores. Conclusion: MMR alone has no stable directional edge on EURUSD M15.

8.4 Related findings that frame the use of both components

  • Sell regime length (EA input, H1 SMA bars). 2023 net: 600 → −4,034; 1,200 (base) → −3,998; 2,400 → −2,767; 4,800 → −1,025. A 2,400-bar regime beat the base in all five years with at least 100 trades (average +$1,555 per year), still losing overall (one profitable year of five). In years when sells are blocked (2013, 2014) it placed only 5 to 7 trades, so “beats the base” there mostly means sitting out.
  • Hurst-trend gate on the 2,400 regime: 2015 −1,700; 2016 −4,858; 2018 −2,437; 2023 −1,023; 2024 +1,134 (profit factor 1.33, drawdown 9.8%, 168 trades).
  • Red zones for sell entries (same sign in every unseen year tested): stochastic K above 80; Williams %R in the overbought third (4 of 5 years); price in the upper 38% of the 5-day range; first-Friday (NFP) days; high ATR; up-and-accelerating EMA phase (5 of 5). Theme: shorts lose when price is strong and accelerating.
  • Interaction scan: 54 tercile-pair cells passed the consistency test versus an average of 5.3 (maximum 12) in 40 shuffled datasets. Calm, orderly tape (low realised volatility, low ATR, low permutation entropy) added about +16 to +26 pts per trade in all five years; high realised volatility subtracted about 15 to 20. These were found and judged on the same five years, so they need an untouched test.
  • Scalping 7–10 point targets at a 5-point commission: hit rates of 70–83% against 91–99% needed to break even; no variant was positive in any of 17 years.

9. Limitations and threats to validity

  • No proven out-of-sample profit. Both components sit inside a system that loses in unseen years at current costs.
  • Fitted constants. ZMax = 1.0, the 0.7/0.3 weights, ZPeriod = 20, the MMR windows and cut-offs were chosen on specific years.
  • Multiple testing. Roughly 60 candidate variants and about 30 replay features were examined; some apparent patterns are luck. Best-of-N results are biased upward.
  • Tick volume is feed-specific; broker and tester differences break the volume term.
  • Survivorship and costs in the stock check (survivor-only universe, no costs, no dividend adjustment, overlapping windows). It is evidence about the direction of the effect, not a trading result.
  • Data. Spread records before 2019 are unusable; history gaps affected earlier results until repaired; the tester models 90% quality bars, not real ticks.
  • MMR proof gate (author’s own standard). Rung 1 (EURUSD, gross of costs): weak and inconsistent. Rung 2 (other markets): partial, daily stocks only. Rungs 3–4 (other brokers and feeds; net of spread, swap and commission variants including MT5 variable spread) are pending. MMR is therefore described as unproven.
  • Swap. Not modelled in the offline lab or the fixed-spread tester. FXOpen offers swap-free Islamic accounts, but its support documentation states the commission equals the swap; a low-swap ECN offer could not be confirmed.
